Product Impact Dashboard

Methodology

How the numbers on the Product Impact Dashboard are collected, defined, and what their limitations are.

Where the data comes from

Each quarter, the Language Technology team compiles a shared "LangTech Analytics Dashboard" spreadsheet, with one row per product. Numbers are self-reported by each product's own team from whatever analytics or telemetry that product collects — there is no single, uniform tracking system across every product. That spreadsheet is converted and published to this dashboard; nothing on this site collects usage data directly.

What each metric means

MetricDefinition
Active Users Distinct people recorded as using the product in the quarter. The exact definition varies by product — for an installed desktop or mobile tool this generally means people actively working in it, while for some web-based products it reflects website visitor traffic rather than people actively using a tool. We're working toward distinguishing these two more clearly on the dashboard itself.
Downloads Installer/package downloads recorded in the quarter, where the product tracks this (not applicable to purely web-based products).
Installs Completed installations recorded in the quarter, where the product tracks this.
Active Projects Distinct projects (e.g. translation projects, dictionaries) with recorded activity in the quarter.
Countries Distinct countries the product recorded usage in during the quarter. The dashboard's "Countries Reached" summary tile shows the single highest per-product count, not a combined total across all products — a true de-duplicated union isn't available since products don't share user-level location data with each other.
Languages Impacted Distinct languages with recorded activity in the product during the quarter.

A dash (—) in the product table means a metric isn't tracked or wasn't reported for that product that quarter — it doesn't mean zero.

Font Usage

Figures in the Font Usage section only count views served through Google Fonts. Not every SIL font is distributed there — see the full catalog at software.sil.org/fonts.

Quarter-over-quarter changes

Where a product's numbers moved notably from the previous quarter, the dashboard's "What changed this quarter" note explains why — for example, a change in how a product measures a metric, versus a genuine change in usage. These notes come from the same quarterly spreadsheet as the metrics themselves, added by whoever reviews that product's numbers.

Known limitations

  • Active Users isn't always apples-to-apples. As noted above, some products report tool usage and others report website traffic under the same metric name.
  • Countries Reached is a maximum, not a union. It reflects whichever single product reached the most countries that quarter, not a combined count across all products.
  • Not every product is developed by SIL Language Technology. A few, like Paratext, are third-party products this team builds extensions for rather than develops outright — flagged directly on the product where this applies.
  • Reporting is self-service and uneven. Since each product team reports its own numbers from whatever analytics it has, coverage and consistency vary product to product and quarter to quarter.
